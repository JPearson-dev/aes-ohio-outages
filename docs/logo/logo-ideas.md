# Project Logo Ideas

Too much time may have been spent on designing a logo. This folder is mainly an archive for general ideas considered, not an exhaustive representation of the design process. Changes to the logo can be considered.

## Final Design

**Description:** Ohio as the body of a two-prong electric plug, with a chart representing an outage imposed on the plug. The style is flat. The chart is yellow to represent the power working, with the area under the curve being darker to represent the power being out. The background is navy, representing nighttime, when a power outage is most obvious. The chart shows a somewhat steep curve, starting high, completing its climb, representing the time when someone would likely start watching outage data. The curve then smoothly decreases over time until it reaches a steady level, representing zero incidents, at which point most people will stop being as interested in the outage.

**Variants:** There are three variants, which differ only in color. At the smallest scale, the two chart colors are less distinct, to better preserve the plug shape, and maybe the Ohio shape. At the mid and large size, the contrast between the two chart colors increase, since the eye can separate the background and dark yellow colors better at those scales. AI thought the mid-size version's max-size should be at 96px, but I think it should probably be around 128px. In any case, I intend to just use the SVG because it is lighterweight and more flexible. So the size distinctions won't be as prominent and can just be eyeballed as to when to use which "size." A more detailed version for Ohio could be used for larger scales.

## Best Ideas

* W1, W2, W3: Stylized map pin with a two-prong power cord and plug coming up from the bottom. Two designs with a pin, one not. The tapered cord doesn't work well. The prongs need to be fully connected to Ohio and may need to be thinner. Ohio might need to be a bit larger.
  * Many iterations deeper, this was my choice. Considered making the prongs and Erie shore look more like smokestacks in a field or towers in a cityscape.
* Classic aluminum can pull-tab: not commonly salient in relation to Dayton. At small size looks like a magnifying glass, map pin, or lightbulb, depending on details. It would be somewhat unique and could support a name like "Dayton Power Pull Tab" or just "The Power Pull Tab"
  * T1, T12: 45 degrees counterclockwise to make more room. Ohio as the interior of the pull ring and a lightning bolt as the pip holding it to the can. Looks a bit lightbulb-ish. T12 has higher detail for Ohio's outline.
  * T6: Ohio as inner and outer shape of the pull ring just changes it into a novelty key blank.
  * T3: A slight tilt to Ohio, distinct from the tab itself, can help it look less like it was just dropped on top of the rest of the design. But it still doesn't look great.
  * M10 has a nice outline with round pip, but it's not right for this project and would need some refinement.
* V1 and V4: A two-blade propeller with Ohio as the hub and a swoosh on the top side to show motion. Interesting, may need adjustment of the blade angle, bolt shape, swoosh shape, and maybe an extra color. Vaguely looks like a coat of arms, which might be a point against it. Likely needs more color in order to work.

## Other Ideas

* Airplane with lightning bolt. This didn't work well, and wasn't legible across multiple attempts. Additionally, fixed-width, blocky lightning bolts with the same style of rectangles imposed on it looks runic in a negative way, or just jumbled.
* A1: lightning bolt in Ohio. Bold, legible, but maybe too simple or uninspired.
  * A4: lightning bolt in Ohio in circle. Still legible, a little less simple, but still not amazing.
* B8 and B9: Vaguely aircraft-like with lightning bolt body, over a circle or Ohio. Interesting, but not really legible. Would work much better if power and power outage don't need to be in context.
* N10: Discarded as too inherently vertical. A traditional ball-and-needle push pin, with a lightning bolt as the pin and Ohio in the ball. It's unclear how much the map pin adds. The bolt connects at a thinner part to the circle, making the bolt clearer and the overall shape less muddled, but doesn't necessarily scale all the way down to favicon size. The right size for Ohio is unclear.
* Wright flyer, but with colored sections to look like a battery. Likely to lose context of the flyer if the colors have too much weight, making the design likely to just not work.
* River theme. Dayton's rivers aren't very recognizable as a shape.
* Bombe rotor (Turing, Desch, NCR): not very recognizable as a shape.
* Radar scope: Wright-Patt. Not otherwise clearly related, but has tracking and awareness connotations.
* Bicycle sprocket: not commonly salient in relation to Dayton, despite Wright Brothers and Huffy
* Synth voltage wave, representing funk music. Too horizontal. Not commonly salient in relation to Dayton.
* Auto-Ignition for cars: commonly cited as related to Dayton, but not really visualizable in an accessible way. Unlikely to scale down well.
* Cash register: wrong iconography
* Cheeze-its: not commonly salient in relation to Dayton. Trademarked.
* Plutonium trigger: I'm not even sure what this looks like, so that's already enough to rule it out.
* Generic electricity options, by themselves: city grid, circuits, lightbulb, lightning bolt
* Generic graph, report, or analysis icons, by themselves
